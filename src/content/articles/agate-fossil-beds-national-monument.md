## Why this place is worth visiting

Agate Fossil Beds National Monument in western Nebraska connects a museum visit with a short trip into the landscape that produced its fossil finds. Inside the visitor center, ancient mammals have shapes you can compare. Outside, grass, bluffs and the Niobrara River wetlands give the site a different scale. The [National Park Service overview](https://www.nps.gov/agfo/index.htm) introduces the fossils and the history of the people who met in this part of the Plains.

## What to see

Start with the visitor center if both themes are new to you. The [park’s things-to-do guide](https://www.nps.gov/agfo/planyourvisit/things2do.htm) describes a display of Miocene mammals and the James Cook Collection. Give the collection time of its own. It includes gifts from Chief Red Cloud and others, which offer a way to learn about the relationships and histories of Lakota and Cheyenne people in this region. Read the interpretation alongside objects that catch your eye, then choose a question to take outside or ask a ranger.

Choose one trail according to what you want to look for. The Daemonelix Trail has exhibits of spiral fossil burrows in place. The Fossil Hills Trail leads toward historic dig sites and crosses the river wetlands. The park describes interpretation signs along that second trail, so make it your choice for the landscape and excavation history. Check the trail descriptions before setting out and leave time to stop at the exhibits along the route you choose.

## Who it suits

Agate works well for families and road-trippers who like a visit built around one exhibit and one walk. Someone curious about how fossils form might spend more time with the burrow exhibits. A traveler interested in Plains history can focus on the collection and read about how the objects came to the site. If your group has different walking needs or limited time, start with the visitor center and decide together how much of the trail to add.

## What to check before going

Use the [official visit planner](https://www.nps.gov/agfo/planyourvisit/index.htm) for directions, operating information, current conditions and accessibility. Check the surface, exposure and return distance for the trail you intend to use. The park advises arriving with fuel and a packed lunch, so make those stops before you leave the main road for the monument. If the weather makes walking uncomfortable, focus the time you have on the visitor center if it is open.
