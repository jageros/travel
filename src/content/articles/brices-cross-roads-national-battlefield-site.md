## Why this place is worth visiting

Brices Cross Roads National Battlefield Site brings the story of a Civil War battle to a small place in northeastern Mississippi. The monument gives you a point to start from. Nearby locations can help you see how much of the story lies beyond that one memorial. The [National Park Service overview](https://www.nps.gov/brcr/index.htm) links to material about the battle and places connected with it. Read a little before arriving so that the ground around the crossroads has meaning when you see it.

## What to see

Begin at the monument if you want a short visit. Take time to look at the memorial and think about what it chooses to commemorate. As you turn back toward the crossroads, notice how the site relates to the roads around it. That is enough for a focused stop on a longer drive, especially if you already spent some time with the history.

For a broader look, use the [official places guide](https://www.nps.gov/brcr/planyourvisit/placestogo.htm) to find out how to get a map of the battlefield driving tour. Decide which places on the map would add to what you learned at the monument. Before you set off, identify the routes and places where you can stop. The guide treats the drive as a way to explore the wider battlefield. Leave time to read about each place you choose and connect it with what you saw at the first stop.

## Who it suits

This stop suits travelers who want to make sense of a historical event by looking at the places connected with it. If your group has limited time or one person is more interested in the history than the others, agree on a short monument visit first. Add the drive when everyone has time to stop and look as well as to cover the route. A family that wants to discuss the battle can choose one question to follow from the monument to a stop on the wider route.

## What to check before going

Check the [official visitor planning page](https://www.nps.gov/brcr/planyourvisit/index.htm) for directions, safety, and accessibility. It explains where to direct questions about the site, which has no visitor facilities. Plan for any services you need before you arrive. If you plan to drive the wider route, confirm the stops, where to park or pull off, and the walking approach at each one. Read signs on site and respect any access limits.
