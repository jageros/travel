## Why this place is worth visiting

At Brown v. Board of Education National Historical Park, you can explore the history of a Supreme Court case inside a school shaped by the very system the case challenged. The former Monroe Elementary School in Topeka served Black children during segregation. In 1954, the Supreme Court ruled that racial segregation in public schools was unconstitutional. Visiting the school gives that national decision a local setting and a way to think about what segregation meant in the lives of students. Start with the [National Park Service overview](https://www.nps.gov/brvb/index.htm) for an introduction to the case and its history.

## What to see

Begin at Monroe Elementary School. Look at the brick exterior before turning to the exhibits described in the [park's things-to-do guide](https://www.nps.gov/brvb/planyourvisit/things2do.htm). Look for the story of public education in Topeka and how it connects to the broader legal challenge. If you want to spend more time there, follow the exhibits at your own pace. Give yourself enough time to read and talk about one story that stays with you before moving on.

For an outdoor stop, use the park guide to find the historic playground or the Landon Trail. A short walk after the exhibits gives your group time to talk about what you learned. Pick one outdoor stop if time or weather is limited.

## Who it suits

The visit suits travelers interested in civil rights, public education or legal history. It works for families whose children are ready to ask what it meant for a school system to separate students by race. Give older children a question to follow through the exhibits, such as what families wanted to change about public education. If people in your group prefer to read at different speeds, choose a place to meet after everyone has explored the exhibits that interest them.

## What to check before going

Use the [official visit planner](https://www.nps.gov/brvb/planyourvisit/index.htm) to confirm hours, directions and current visitor information for the Topeka site. Review the accessibility guidance and contact the park if you need details about a specific route or facility. If a guided activity matters to your group, check the current schedule and any arrangements in advance. For an outdoor stop, check local weather and the trail or grounds information. If you have only a short visit, make the school and exhibits your first stop.
