BART and Muni are transport services. Clipper is a way to pay for eligible journeys or hold eligible products. A Visitor Passport is a specific Muni product. Keep these names separate and you can avoid buying a city pass that does not cover your airport journey.

Begin with the service named in your journey planner, then check the product and payment method. One itinerary may include more than one operator even when the same card is accepted for payment.

## Know which name answers which question

| Name | What it tells you | What to check next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BART | The regional rail journey, including an SFO connection when shown | Stations, direction and the fare for that journey |
| Muni | The city service: bus, Muni Metro, historic streetcar or cable car | Which fare product applies to that vehicle |
| Clipper | A payment system and a way to hold eligible products | Whether you have stored value or a particular pass |
| Contactless bank card | A payment method for supported adult fares | Eligibility, transfers and use of the same card or device |
| Visitor Passport | A Muni pass with specified coverage and validity | Whether its included journeys match your plan |

Use [BART's SFO guide](https://www.bart.gov/guide/airport/sfo) for the airport rail journey and [SFMTA's fare overview](https://www.sfmta.com/getting-around/muni/fares) for Muni. The planner's change of operator is your cue to check coverage again.

## A balance, a pass and a bank-card tap are different

Stored value pays eligible individual fares. A pass covers the services and validity stated in its conditions. Adding money to a card does not prove that you have bought a pass; tapping a bank card does not automatically buy a Visitor Passport.

| Proposed ride | Coverage boundary to check |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary Muni bus, Muni Metro or F-line historic streetcar | Compare an applicable ordinary single fare with a Muni-only day product |
| Cable car | Check a cable-car ticket or a product that explicitly includes cable cars |
| BART airport journey | Check BART separately; a Muni Visitor Passport is not an SFO airport ticket |
| Another operator in the same itinerary | Read that operator's fare conditions rather than assuming a Muni product applies |

[SFMTA's Visitor Passport page](https://www.sfmta.com/fares/1-day-visitor-passport) defines its Muni coverage and exclusions. A cable car and a historic streetcar are different fare decisions, even though both may be part of a sightseeing day.

## Count transfers without guessing the credit

A change of vehicle is not always another full fare. An ordinary Muni fare can cover eligible transfers within its valid window, as explained on [the single-ride page](https://www.sfmta.com/fares/single-ride-adult-ages-19-64). Count paid fare windows, then check whether an inter-operator credit applies to the actual journey.

For a cross-operator connection, verify the eligible operator pair, time condition and payment method. Do not assume two full fares are always required, or that a shared card makes the second service free. Keep proof of payment when required and use the same payment identity where the rules depend on it.

## Make one payment plan per rider

[Clipper's contactless guide](https://www.clippercard.com/contactless-payments) explains adult bank-card fares, separate rider payment and entry/exit consistency. A plastic card, phone and watch are different payment presentations; do not switch between them during a journey that requires consistent entry and exit taps.

On BART, complete the required entry and exit taps with the same method. Muni has its own boarding and fare-gate instructions: [SFMTA explains that Muni Metro does not require a tap out](https://www.sfmta.com/fares/single-ride-adult-ages-19-64). Do not apply one operator's gate behavior to another.

If someone qualifies for a discount, check the appropriate program and payment method. An ordinary bank-card tap should not be assumed to apply a concession. Arrange a suitable backup if a phone battery or card setup could interrupt the journey.

## Choose the product for the actual day

Put airport travel, ordinary Muni fare windows, cable car rides and other operators on separate lines. Then use the [pass comparison](/guides/is-muni-pass-worth-it/) for the journeys a Muni product covers. For an airport arrival with bags, apply those boundaries to the [SFO-to-Wharf transfer](/guides/sfo-to-fishermans-wharf-with-luggage/).

For an ordinary single fare, follow the current Clipper or contactless-bank-card instructions linked above. For a [Day Pass](https://www.sfmta.com/fares/day-pass) or Visitor Passport, check its dedicated product page for the available purchase channel and activation. Do not assume that a single-ride option named in an older app instruction is still sold. This guide does not sell or activate tickets.
