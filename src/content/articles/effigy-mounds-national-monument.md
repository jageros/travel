## Why this place is worth visiting

At Effigy Mounds, an earthen mound can look like a slight rise in the woods. The visitor center gives you the context to recognize what you are seeing and to understand why the site matters. The mounds are part of a sacred Indigenous burial and ceremonial landscape. Start with the [National Park Service overview](https://www.nps.gov/efmo/index.htm) to learn why the construction of animal-shaped earthworks mattered in this region. Once you reach the trail, pause on it to notice the outlines, the space between mounds and their setting on the bluff.

## What to see

Make the **visitor center** your first stop. The [park's trail and exhibit guide](https://www.nps.gov/efmo/planyourvisit/things2do.htm) describes exhibits on the monument's cultural and natural history and maps for the hikes. Use the exhibits to go beyond identifying shapes. Pick up a map or review the route descriptions before choosing your walk.

If your group wants the mounds and a broad river view on the same walk, look at the **Fire Point Loop** in the North Unit. The route described by the park passes the Little Bear and Great Bear mounds and has overlooks of the Mississippi. Allow time to stop along the way. The **Yellow River Boardwalk** has a different focus. It crosses marsh and river terrain, so it offers a look at the landscape below the bluffs. You can choose it for a lower-elevation outdoor visit and learn about the mounds through the visitor center.

## Who it suits

The monument suits visitors who like a walk with time to learn the history of a place. Fire Point is a good match for a group interested in the earthworks and comfortable with a sustained climb. If your group wants to limit climbing, plan around the visitor center and consider the boardwalk separately. Read the access information for the particular stop you plan to visit. The conditions at a river boardwalk and at a bluff overlook can be quite different.

## What to check before going

Use the [official trip planner](https://www.nps.gov/efmo/planyourvisit/index.htm) to check visitor center information and confirm access to your chosen route. The [park's safety guide](https://www.nps.gov/efmo/planyourvisit/safety.htm) describes steep bluff trails and boardwalks that can be slippery when wet. Check the weather and footing before deciding between the walks, and stay back from overlook edges. Leave time to learn at the visitor center if conditions make the walk you had in mind a poor fit.
