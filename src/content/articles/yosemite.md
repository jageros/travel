## Why this place is worth visiting

Yosemite Valley rewards a change of viewpoint. From above, you can read the broad shape of the valley, with cliffs on either side and layers of granite fading into the distance. From the valley floor, one rock face can fill your field of view. Build a first visit around those two perspectives, then let the weather and the season decide which details to spend more time on.

## What to see

Start with **Tunnel View** if it fits your entrance route. On a clear day, look for El Capitan on the left, Bridalveil Fall to the right and Half Dome farther back. The [National Park Service valley guide](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/yv.htm) explains where the turnout sits relative to the Wawona Tunnel. Orient everyone in your group before moving on so the features are easier to pick out at the next stop.

Choose one complementary valley-floor stop. At **El Capitan Meadow**, the cliff face dominates the view. **Valley View** frames the granite from alongside the Merced River, with water and foreground vegetation changing the composition. The valley guide places these stops along one-way roads, so match the stop to your direction when planning. In spring, the park's [overview](https://www.nps.gov/yose/index.htm) points visitors toward snowmelt-fed waterfalls. Look up conditions before building a trip around strong flow.

## Who it suits

This pair of viewpoints works for a first-timer interested in big scenery, a group splitting its attention between photographs and walking, or someone fitting Yosemite into a longer trip. Take time to look at El Capitan up close after seeing it from the overlook. If your group wants to keep walking, select a valley walk separately and verify its approach, surface and length for the people coming with you.

## What to check before going

Open the [current conditions page](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/conditions.htm) for road status, trail updates and seasonal changes. Confirm entry arrangements through the park's current guidance, then plug in your specific entrance and valley stop. Leave margin for traffic and parking. Check the current shuttle or bus information if either is part of the plan.

A second stop is optional when visibility or traffic changes. Keep the day centered on a place you can reach safely, and use one-way route information to make sure your intended return takes you where you want to go.
