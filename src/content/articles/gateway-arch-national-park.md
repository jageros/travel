## Why this place is worth visiting

The Gateway Arch can look like a clean line on the St. Louis skyline. At ground level, you can see the distance between its legs, the way the curve narrows as it rises and the relationship between the monument, the city and the Mississippi River. That change in perspective is worth a walk around the grounds. The [National Park Service overview](https://www.nps.gov/jeff/index.htm) introduces the Arch and the Old Courthouse. The museum beneath the Arch adds a third focus to the visit.

## What to see

Start on the **Arch grounds**. Step back far enough to see the full curve, then walk closer and look up from near one leg. Find a spot that lets you look toward the river, too. If you want context for the monument and the city around it, use the [official Gateway Arch guide](https://www.nps.gov/jeff/planyourvisit/gateway-arch.htm) to plan time in the underground museum. Decide separately whether to take the tram to the observation area. It adds a view from inside the Arch and has its own boarding arrangements.

If the history of the city is your main interest, consider the **Old Courthouse**. The [park's history guide](https://www.nps.gov/jeff/learn/historyculture/index.htm) explains the courthouse's connection to Dred Scott's case. Read it in advance so you can place the courtrooms and exhibits in context if the building is available for your visit. With limited time, give the Arch grounds and one indoor stop enough room in your itinerary before adding another.

## Who it suits

This visit suits people interested in architecture, city and river views, or the history of St. Louis. For a group with different plans, agree on a shared place to view the Arch, then decide who wants to add the museum, courthouse or tram. If stairs, enclosed spaces or standing time matter to anyone, consult the [park's accessibility guide](https://www.nps.gov/jeff/planyourvisit/accessibility.htm) for the particular experience before buying tickets.

## What to check before going

Use the [official visit planner](https://www.nps.gov/jeff/planyourvisit/index.htm) for current hours, access, directions and ticket information. Check the entrance you need rather than heading straight for one of the legs. If you are taking the tram, confirm availability and leave time for security and check-in. Check courthouse access separately before making it the focus of the visit. If the tram is unavailable or unsuitable, make time on the grounds and pick an indoor stop that works for your group.
