## Why this place is worth visiting

At Adams National Historical Park in Quincy, two nearby birthplaces and a later family estate show how one family lived across generations. Begin by deciding what you want to compare. The houses at Penn's Hill tell an early chapter in the lives of John and John Quincy Adams. The Old House at Peacefield gives you another setting in which to look at the wider family. The [National Park Service overview](https://www.nps.gov/adam/index.htm) leads to material on the homes, people and collections.

## What to see

Pick the homes at Penn's Hill if you want to focus on the early years of the two presidents. The houses are close together, so even a short visit can give you time to compare their shapes and the way each sits on the ground. Read the [park's things-to-do guide](https://www.nps.gov/adam/planyourvisit/things2do.htm) for the park's tour options. Give yourself a little time after any visit inside to look back at the exteriors with what you have just learned in mind.

At Peacefield, think about how the Old House and grounds work together. The formal garden and the orchard give you places to pause if those parts of the property are open. If you want to begin with more context, see whether the orientation film at the visitor center is available. It can help you put the people and places in order before you go to the houses.

## Who it suits

This visit suits travelers who like early American history, household stories or historic architecture. A group with mixed interests can choose one property as the anchor, then discuss whether to see the other. If standing through a tour or moving between stops would be tiring, build a shorter plan around the visitor center or one property. Look at the park's virtual tours if an interior you want to see is unavailable when you go.

## What to check before going

The [official visitor planner](https://www.nps.gov/adam/planyourvisit/index.htm) links to directions, access information, operating hours and details about admission and tours. Look up the specific building and grounds you want to see, because a plan for one part of the park may not fit another. Confirm where to meet, how to travel between properties and which entrances work for your group. If your time is limited, choose one stop and leave room to walk around it before moving on.
