San Francisco's cable cars and F-line historic streetcars are different rides. If you want the cable-car experience, look for California, Powell/Hyde or Powell/Mason in the official route information. If you want the historic streetcar serving the waterfront, look for F Market & Wharves. A vintage appearance alone does not tell you which system to use.

Choose the ride first, then check whether it serves the journey you want. Buying a ticket product before making that distinction can leave you planning around the wrong vehicle or boarding point.

## Match the name to the experience

[SFMTA's Cable Cars page](https://www.sfmta.com/getting-around/muni/cable-cars) describes cars pulled by cables running beneath the street and names three cable-car lines. [Its F Market & Wharves page](https://www.sfmta.com/routes/f-market-wharves) provides a separate route and stop list for the historic streetcar, including the Ferry Building, Pier 39 and Wharf area.

| What you are trying to find | Name to check in official information | A useful way to confirm your choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A cable-car ride | California, Powell/Hyde or Powell/Mason | Open that named cable-car line from SFMTA's Cable Cars page |
| A historic streetcar along the waterfront | F Market & Wharves | Find the F-line stop for the destination you intend to visit |
| A practical connection between two places | The route shown for your complete journey | Confirm the boarding point, direction and destination instead of choosing by appearance |

The photograph on this page shows an F-line historic streetcar. It illustrates the type of ride discussed here; it is not a cable car or a promise that this particular vehicle will operate when you visit. Use the route name and destination information to identify a service rather than trying to match a photograph's color or fleet number.

## Decide whether the ride or the destination comes first

If riding a cable car is the main activity, choose one of the named cable-car lines and inspect its official route information. Work out how you will reach its boarding point and continue after leaving it. A stop somewhere in the same neighborhood does not establish that it is the right line for your plans.

If the waterfront is the main destination, begin with the F-line stop list. Locate the Ferry Building, Pier 39 or the particular Wharf destination you want, then inspect the correct direction. Our [waterfront streetcar outing](/guides/sf-waterfront-transit-itinerary/) helps turn those stops into a flexible visit with an optional extension.

If you want both experiences, write them as two separate journey decisions. Check each mode's route and boarding information.

## Check a boarding point before going there

For cable cars, SFMTA directs riders to the turntables at the ends of the routes or to brown-and-white cable-car signposts. Use the official page's named line links to confirm which route you are looking at.

For the F line, open the route page and choose the direction before selecting a stop. Its two directional lists are separate. Save the stop you will use to return as well as the place where you plan to arrive; do not assume they are the same location.

Before leaving, record these details together:

- The mode and exact line name you intend to ride.
- The boarding location and direction for the outward journey.
- The stop or destination where you intend to leave that service.
- The separately checked return boarding point.

Check current operator information again on the day of travel. A route map identifies the planned route; it does not guarantee a particular departure, waiting time or available place on a vehicle.

## Resolve boarding needs before choosing the experience

SFMTA states that cable cars are not equipped with accessible boarding. If that limitation affects someone in your group, resolve the transport choice before committing to a cable-car outing. Do not treat a historic streetcar photograph, or the presence of an F-line stop near a destination, as evidence that another journey meets your access needs.

Ask the operator about the exact boarding and exit points relevant to your trip. This guide identifies the modes; it has not verified the condition of a boarding point or equipment for your travel date. If a required detail remains unconfirmed, choose a journey you can verify rather than assuming a nearby mode is interchangeable.

## Compare tickets after the route is clear

Keep two questions separate: which ride do you want, and which product covers the journeys you will actually make? The [Muni pass comparison](/guides/is-muni-pass-worth-it/) explains how to compare ordinary paid fare windows, cable-car tickets and a cable-car-inclusive product.

Check the current product's coverage and purchase conditions before buying.
